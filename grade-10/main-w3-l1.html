Designing a Solution + Build, Test, Improve

Today you become a designer. You take one specific task and design a simple AI workflow – a set of ordered steps – that completes it, then run it and explain exactly why it gave the result it did. Then, in the Lab, you test the workflow, fix its weakest step, and grow it into a multi-step solution that combines two tools and two data sources – and prove it completes the task reliably.

A solution is an ordered workflow

A simple AI solution is not one magic step – it is a workflow of ordered steps. You give the first step an input, it produces an output, and that output becomes the input of the next step, until the last step completes your task. At least one step uses an AI tool, but the steps around it – preparing the input, sorting the output – are what make the solution actually work. Because the steps are ordered, each depends on the one before it, so the sequence you choose is part of the design.

Core · Part 2 of 4

Prepare the input, then explain the result

What happens before and after the AI step?

Prepare and check the input first

The step before the AI tool matters as much as the tool. A prepare step cleans and checks the input – remove anything personal, fix obvious errors, and make sure the format is what the next step expects. Rubbish in, rubbish out: a good input check stops a small problem becoming a wrong appointment. In the clinic helper, the prepare step also strips any name or ID so no personal data reaches the AI tool.

Design the steps, then explain the result

Systems thinking means breaking one specific task into steps before you build: what does each step need, and what should it produce. Once you have run the workflow, you explain the result by tracing the final output back to the step that produced it – that is the reasoning behind it. Then you verify before you trust: check the output against the task and a trusted source. Even a confident output can be wrong, so if it fails you already know which step to check.

Why it matters

Across the UAE, organisations build simple AI workflows to sort requests, screen documents and draft summaries – always as ordered steps, tested before anyone trusts them. (Illustrative scenarios.) Learning to design and explain one now means you can shape these solutions with systems thinking, not just use them.

Lab · Part 1 of 3

Improve one step, then combine

How do you make a build better – and prove it?

Improve by changing one step at a time

The fastest way to improve a solution is not to rebuild it. You test it on fresh input, find the one step that weakens the result – often the prepare step or the AI tool step – and change only that. Because the steps are ordered, that single change ripples to every step after it, so keeping it to one change is how you know which change helped. Then you run it again and compare against the task. Notes of before and after turn guessing into evidence. This is iteration: a controlled loop that makes your build better and lets you explain exactly why.

Combine tools and data to do more

A stronger solution combines more than one tool and more than one source of data. The output of the first tool step becomes the input of the second – that join is the hand-off – and a second data source adds information the first did not have. So when you test a multi-step solution you check three things: does each step do its part, do the joins between steps and data sources hold, and does the whole workflow complete the task. A solution is reliable only when it holds across more than one input, so always test with at least two, and verify each output before you trust it.

Why it matters

Real UAE solutions – screening documents, routing requests, planning logistics – are built as workflows that combine several tools and data sources, and are tested across many inputs before anyone trusts or governs them. (Illustrative scenarios.) Building and testing a multi-step solution now is this professional skill in miniature.

Lesson summary

Design it as ordered steps – then test, improve one step, and prove it is reliable

  • A simple AI solution is a workflow of ordered steps; the sequence is part of the design.
  • The prepare step cleans and checks the input – and removes personal data – before the AI step.
  • Explain a result by tracing the output back to the step that produced it.
  • Verify before you trust: check the final output against the task and a trusted source.
  • Improve by changing one step at a time, then compare against the task – evidence, not guessing.
  • A multi-step solution combines more than one tool and data source through clean hand-offs. Test the parts, the joins, and the whole task.
  • Reliable means it holds across more than one input – so test with at least two, and verify each output.
