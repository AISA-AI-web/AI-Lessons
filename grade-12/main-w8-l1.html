Build to your plan

Building is not inventing something new – it is assembling the components you already designed, in the order your plan sets out. Add one component at a time and run it before you connect the next. That way, if something breaks, you know exactly which part caused it. Remember the rules of the room: the AI step is run by your teacher on an approved tool, and you never put anyone's personal details, names or faces into any input. Sample, non-personal data only. If the tool is unavailable, your paper pipeline does the same job.

Core · Part 2 of 3 · Worksheet

Let the evidence decide

A fluent, well-written answer can still be wrong.

Once your solution runs end to end, evaluate it properly. Put each output next to the acceptance criterion it is meant to meet and mark pass or fail – on the evidence, not on how confident it sounds. Then take one confident-looking output and check it against a trusted source, because a fluent, well-written answer can still be wrong. Pick the single weakest link, make one change, and run it again to see if the score improved. Changing one thing at a time is how you know what actually helped, and one solid, evidence-based improvement beats ten guesses.

Why it matters

Across the UAE, teams shipping AI systems – from smart-city services to healthcare triage – succeed by building in stages and evaluating hard against criteria before anyone relies on the output. The same discipline you use today is what keeps real strategic systems safe and trustworthy.

Cross-curricular link · Mathematics · Design & Technology

Marking each output pass or fail against an acceptance criterion is measurement against a standard in Mathematics. Assembling from a plan and iterating is the make-and-test cycle from Design & Technology.

Lab · Part 1 of 3

Finish it and log it

A couple of spot checks is not an evaluation.

The Lab goal is a solution that runs from input to output on its own, plus a full evaluation log – not just a couple of spot checks. Log as you build: every output next to its criterion, marked pass or fail, with a note on how you verified it. Be honest about failures, because the failures are what you learn from. The room rules still stand: the AI step is teacher-run on an approved tool, and only sample, non-personal data goes in. If tools are down, your paper pipeline does the same work and the log looks the same.

Why it matters

The AI systems that UAE organisations trust for strategic work are judged not only on their best-case output but on how safely they handle the unexpected. Learning to complete, log and harden a solution is exactly the design responsibility a real AI systems designer carries.

Cross-curricular link · Mathematics · Design & Technology

An evaluation log of repeated runs is a data table read for trends in Mathematics. Designing for the edge case is robust design from Design & Technology.

Tool for this week: school-approved generative assistant (build + evaluate), teacher-mediated · optional Colab

Feed a deliberate edge case, make it fail gracefully, then improve a second weak link and compare before and after. Stay safe: teacher-operated tool; non-personal inputs only. A graceful fallback never exposes data or gives a confident wrong answer.

Lab · Part 3 of 3 · Unplugged

Make it handle the unexpected

Fail gracefully – never with a confident wrong answer.

Real systems meet inputs nobody planned for – blank, strange or extreme (an edge case) – and that is where wrong answers slip through. So test on purpose: feed your solution an edge case or an incorrect input and watch what it does. If it crashes or confidently gives a wrong answer, fix it so it fails gracefully – for example, returning ‘unknown, refer to a person’ instead of a bad guess. Then iterate on a second weak link. For every change, compare the evaluation before and after on the same inputs, so you can prove the change actually helped rather than just hoping it did. Document each fix: what broke, what you changed, what improved.

Lesson summary

Build it to plan, let evidence decide – then finish it and harden it

  • Building is assembling your planned components in order – one at a time, checked before the next.
  • Evaluate each output against its acceptance criterion – pass/fail on evidence.
  • A confident output can be wrong – verify one against a trusted source.
  • Improve one component, then re-run.
  • Complete every component so the system runs end to end, and keep an evaluation log – repeated scored runs make improvement evidence.
  • Handle the edge case: fail gracefully, never a confident wrong answer – and document each fix: what broke, what you changed, what improved.
