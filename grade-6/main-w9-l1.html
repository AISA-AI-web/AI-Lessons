Judging your solution three ways

A good builder does not just ask ‘do I like it?’. You measure your work against the criteria the class agreed.

Accuracy means testing with examples where you already know the right answer. Usefulness means checking it really helps your real user. Risk means asking what could go wrong and whether any private data is exposed.

Core · Part 2 of 3

Real quality checks

What does a true check look like?

Figure 2 · A real check gives evidence

✅ Real checks: 🧪 test on known answers (accuracy) · 🙋 ask your real user (usefulness) · 🔒 look for exposed private data (risk).
❌ Not checks: 🤷 ‘Do I like it?’ · 🎨 ‘Is the colour nice?’ – they tell you nothing about how the solution decides.

Genuine checks, not opinions

A quality check gives you evidence. Testing on known answers gives evidence about accuracy. Asking your real user gives evidence about usefulness. Looking for exposed data gives evidence about risk.

Asking ‘do I like it?’ or ‘is the colour nice?’ tells you nothing about how the solution decides – so it is not a real check.

Core · Part 3 of 3

One change, proven

Make it, then prove it helped.

One change, proven

You do not need many changes. Choose the one improvement that matters most to your user.

Make the change, then re-test. If accuracy went up and nothing else broke, you have evidence it worked. A change that fixes one case can quietly break another, so always re-test before you call it done.

Judge, improve, and prove it

Work with your partner. Judge your solution against the rubric, choose one improvement, make it, and prove it helped.

Let's try it

What you need: printed rubric and criteria card · refinement-note sheet · known-answer test cases · a pencil.

  1. Read the three lenses on the criteria card.
  2. Run one accuracy test with known answers and record the score.
  3. Check usefulness against your real user’s task.
  4. Do a basic risk check for harm, fairness and privacy.
  5. Pick one improvement, make it, and re-test to confirm it helped.
  6. Swap with another pair for ‘two stars and a wish’ feedback.

Lab · Part 1 of 3

Finish, then freeze

How do you lock in your best version?

Finish, then freeze

Make your final planned change and re-test with known answers. Record before and after so others can trust your evidence.

Then freeze the version. The solution you show at the carousel must be the exact one you tested and judged safe.

Lab · Part 2 of 3

Sort the risks

How likely, and how serious?

Sorting risks the smart way

Not every risk is equal. A risk that is both likely and serious needs action now. A rare but very serious risk still needs a plan.

Place each risk on the matrix, then decide: fix it now, or watch it. Always confirm no personal data is anywhere in your solution.

Lab · Part 3 of 3

Safe and fair

Match each risk to its fix.

Figure L3 · Every common AI risk has a matching fix

🎯 Wrong answer → re-test on known answers · ⚖️ Unfair result → balance the example data · 🔒 Private data exposed → remove it · 🔧 Broke after a change → re-test every case.

From risk to fix

Common risks for AI-supported solutions include a wrong answer that misleads a user, unfair treatment of one group because the example data was unbalanced, and exposure of private information.

Each risk has a fix. A wrong answer means re-test on known answers; unfairness means balance the data; exposed data means remove it. Then you can write your safe-and-fair statement with confidence.

Lab mission · Worksheet

Final change, full risk review, freeze

With your partner, make the final change, re-test, run the full risk review, and freeze your solution ready for showcase.

Let's try it

What you need: printed risk matrix · final-iteration log · five known-answer test cases · sticky notes · a pencil.

  1. Set your Lab goal from the refinement log.
  2. Make the final planned improvement.
  3. Re-test with at least five known-answer cases and record before and after.
  4. List possible risks and place each one on the matrix.
  5. Decide actions and confirm no personal data is used anywhere.
  6. Write a safe-and-fair statement and freeze the version.
  7. Swap matrices with another pair to sense-check placements.

Tool for this week: your frozen solution, the paper risk matrix and the final-iteration log

Re-test only with known-answer cases and made-up examples. Never put personal or identifying data into a prompt or training set – if you find any, remove it before you freeze. Freeze the exact version you tested, and do not edit it after that. No device or board? Lesson summary

You judged your own work like a detective, proved one change made it better, and showed your solution is safe and fair before freezing it

Key ideas to remember

  • Refining means measuring your solution against the agreed criteria, not against how you feel about it.
  • Judge three ways: accuracy (test on known answers), usefulness (help the real user), and risk (harm, fairness, privacy).
  • One well-chosen improvement, backed by evidence, beats many random changes.
  • Always re-test after a change, because a fix for one case can quietly break another.
  • A risk review weighs how likely a risk is against how serious it would be: likely-and-serious risks need fixing now; rare-but-serious risks still need a plan; small risks can be watched.
  • Never put personal or identifying data into a prompt or training set, and freeze the exact version you tested.

Glossary

Accuracy
how often the solution gives the right or sensible answer, checked on known examples.
Usefulness
how well the solution really helps the real user do their task.
Risk
what could go wrong, who could be harmed, and whether private data is exposed.
Refinement note
a short record of the change you made and the evidence it helped.
Risk review
a structured check of what could go wrong before people rely on a solution.
Risk matrix
a grid that weighs how likely a risk is against how serious it would be.
Final iteration
your last planned change, re-tested and recorded with before-and-after evidence.
Freeze
to stop changing a solution so the version shown is the one you tested.
