A truthful pitch

What belongs in an honest pitch?

Previously

In Week 9 you refined and finished your signature solution, then froze it. Today you show it to others – and tell the truth about it.

Words to know

Tap a card to flip it and reveal the meaning.

A pitch that tells the truth

A strong pitch names your user, the problem, how your solution works, and the evidence that it is accurate, useful and safe.

Explain your choices by giving reasons, not just descriptions. Be honest about one limit. Honesty makes your work more trustworthy, not weaker.

Show the whole term

The short assessment lets you show all four strands: understanding, designing and building, evaluating and responsible use, and wider effects.

For each strand you can point to real evidence from your term – the parts you built, the tests you ran, and who your solution helps.

Core mission · Worksheet

Assess, pitch, and set your goal

Complete the short assessment, then present your solution and justify two choices before setting your next-term goal.

Tool for this week: unplugged – printed assessment task, pitch card and four-strand summary poster

No logins and no live tool. Any AI you show stays teacher-run on the board. You present your frozen solution from your pitch card and answer the assessment on paper. Stay safe: never put a real name, photo, home or age into a tool – use made-up (synthetic) data only. Be honest about limits. Cannot present live? Record your pitch on the pitch card and swap cards with a peer for silent written feedback.

Let's try it

What you need: printed assessment task, pitch card, four-strand summary poster, a pencil.

  1. Read the four-strand summary and set out your evidence.
  2. Complete the short Term-1 assessment task on your own.
  3. Prepare a 60-second pitch naming user, problem and two justified choices.
  4. Present to your small group and answer one ‘why’ question.
  5. Give one peer a specific ‘why’ question about their choices.
  6. Write one thing learned and one Term-2 goal toward Proficient.

Lab · Part 1 of 3

Present at your station

How does a showcase carousel work?

The showcase flow

In the carousel you present at your own station in 60 seconds, then visit other stations. Everyone rotates, so the whole class presents and gives feedback in a short time.

When you present, name two choices you made and give a reason for each. A clear, honest 60 seconds beats reading out every step.

Lab · Part 2 of 3

Feedback that helps

What does helpful feedback sound like?

Two stars and a wish

At each station you leave two stars and a wish: two things that worked and one idea to improve. Tag each comment to a strand so the builder knows where it fits.

Good feedback is specific, kind, and tied to evidence. A helpful wish is a gift, not a criticism – it points to a real change the builder could make.

Lab · Part 3 of 3

Your term in one picture

What can you now do across the four strands?

Consolidate and confirm

Consolidating means gathering the small notes you made all term into one clear picture across the four strands: concepts, design and build, evaluate and use, and wider effects.

Then set one specific Term-2 goal aimed at Proficient, and confirm every solution on display is responsible: it uses no personal data and is honest about its limits.

Lab mission · Worksheet

Showcase carousel: present, feedback, and reflect

Turn the room into a gallery. Present your solution, leave honest strand-tagged feedback at other stations, then pull your term into one four-strand summary and a single Term-2 goal.

Tool for this week: unplugged – station cards, feedback slips and a four-strand summary sheet

The carousel needs no devices and no accounts. Your frozen solution, its evidence and its responsible-use message sit on a station card. Keep it private: we never type a real name, photo, home or age into a tool, and nothing personal goes on display – made-up (synthetic) data only. Once information goes into a system it is hard to take back. No live carousel? Run a paper gallery: lay out station cards and evidence, circulate silently leaving written feedback slips, then consolidate the same way.

Let's try it

What you need: station cards, feedback slips, four-strand summary sheet, a pencil.

  1. Set up your station with your frozen solution, evidence and responsible-use message.
  2. Present in 60 seconds, naming two justified choices.
  3. Rotate to at least two stations and leave ‘two stars and a wish’ tagged to a strand.
  4. Collect the feedback slips left at your own station.
  5. Gather your notes and feedback onto the four-strand summary.
  6. Set one specific Term-2 goal aimed at Proficient, and confirm your responsible-use message is honest.

Fair or unfair? A quick story

We train a helper with many cat photos but very few dogs. Shown a new dog, it says ‘cat’. No one wrote a wrong rule – the training data was unbalanced, so the model learned a biased pattern. Balanced, clean training data gives fairer, more accurate answers.

Key ideas to remember

  • A clear pitch names the user, the problem, how the solution works, and the evidence it is accurate, useful and safe.
  • Explaining a choice means giving the reason behind it, not just describing what you did.
  • The short assessment shows all four strands: understanding, building, evaluating and responsible use, and wider effects.
  • A showcase carousel lets a whole class present and give feedback in a short time.
  • Good peer feedback is specific, kind, tied to evidence, and tagged to a strand.
  • Consolidating means gathering your term notes into one four-strand picture and one goal.
  • Responsible communicators are honest about limits, use no personal data, and set a specific goal toward the next tier.

Glossary

Pitch

Design or evaluation choice
a decision you made and the reason behind it, not just a description.
Four strands
understanding, designing and building, evaluating and responsible use, and wider effects.
Proficient
the next tier of skill you aim toward with your goal.
Showcase carousel
a way for a class to present at stations while everyone rotates and gives feedback.
Two stars and a wish
two things that worked plus one idea to improve, tagged to a strand.
Consolidate
gather your small term notes into one clear four-strand picture.
Responsible-use message
a note that a solution uses no personal data and is honest about its limits.
